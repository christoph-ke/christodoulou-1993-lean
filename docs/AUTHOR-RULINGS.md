# Approved interpretive rulings

These conventions were approved for this formalization on 2026-10-17. They specify how to read the frozen Lean definitions and statements where the paper leaves a convention implicit or the formalized scope differs. The additional Lemma 6.2 rulings are dated separately. They are decisions about this formalization, not claims of approval by the author of the 1993 paper. See the [README](../README.md), [certification](../CERTIFICATION.md), and [errata](../ERRATA.md) for the resulting statements and checks.

**D2 — Field carrier.** Use seven total real-valued functions $r,\phi,m,\lambda,\nu,\alpha,\beta$ of double-null coordinates. Derive $\theta=\alpha-\lambda\phi$ and $\zeta=\beta-\nu\phi$. Write $\mu=2m/r$ inline in the equations; do not store a separate conformal factor. The conformal relation (1.5) is instead a positive pointwise witness in the solution predicate. Values outside the solution domain have no imposed physical meaning.

**D3 — Domains of development.** Use the printed causal past $J^-(a,b)=\{(u,v):0\le u\le a,\ u\lt v\lt b\}$ (p. 1146), with an open upper endpoint in $v$. A domain of development is a subset of $\{0\le u\lt v\}$ containing the causal past of every one of its points. Do not add openness or connectedness; empty or irregular sets are allowed by this definition.

**D4 — Meaning of solution.** Use the reduced system (1.12), (1.14), (1.15), (1.17), and (1.18), integrated on every contained closed null segment. Represent the axis conditions (1.7) by outgoing limits of $r$ and $m$ to zero. Require $r\gt0$, $\lambda\gt0$, and a positive conformal witness satisfying (1.5). This is the setting used for the BV and C¹ results, not a derivation from the four-dimensional Einstein equations. The inequalities $\nu\lt0$ and $1-2m/r\gt0$ are not additional fields of the definition.

**D5 — BV class and axis conventions.** Use the six conditions on pp. 1146–1147 with constants uniform over the whole domain, a uniform lower bound for $\nu$, and a uniform positive lower bound for $\lambda$. Total variation is pointwise `eVariationOn` on the relevant slices intersected with the domain. Impose no right-continuity convention. Axis points are diagonal points in the closure of the domain; outgoing axis limits can be vacuous at terminal corners with empty outgoing slices. BV item 4 retains all four printed parameter ranges, alternating $0\lt\delta\le\varepsilon$ and $0\le\delta\lt\varepsilon$.

**D6 — C¹ class.** Use the reduced-system solution predicate and the same uniform expansion bounds. Require $\lambda,\nu,\phi,\theta,\zeta$ to be C¹ on the closure of the domain, as on p. 1157, using Lean's relative regularity predicate. The regularity requirement is on the closure, including its axis points; the equations themselves are imposed off the axis.

**D7 — Initial data and gauge.** For uniqueness, compare the initial radius $r$ and scalar field $\phi$. In the continuation and small-data existence statements use the gauge $r(0,v)=v/2$ and prescribe $\alpha(0,v)=\alpha_0(v)$, and measure small-data size by the total variation of $\alpha_0$ on $(0,\infty)$. Do not add the normalization $\phi(0,0)=0$ as a theorem hypothesis; the paper treats scalar normalization as a choice available in a proof.

**D8 — Initial edge of developments.** Take the global domain to be $\{(u,v):0\le u\lt v\}$. For finite developments use $\{(u,v):0\le u\lt v\lt v_0\}$. These sets include the characteristic initial edge $u=0$, where the data live, unlike the printed open triangles. Retain a separate definition of the literal open development $\{(u,v):u_0\lt u\lt v\lt v_0\}$.

**D9 — Deferred area conclusions.** Freeze and prove the existence fragment of Theorems 6.1 and 6.2. Defer the four multiplicity-counted image-area finiteness conclusions in each printed theorem: the images under $(\lambda,\phi)$, $(\nu,\phi)$, $(\phi,\alpha/\lambda)$, and $(\phi,\beta/\nu)$. Adding those conclusions requires a later statement with an audited multiplicity-area definition, retaining a shared $\varepsilon_*$ for the two branches. Analytic area estimates inside the present proofs do not constitute these omitted conclusions.

**Theorem 3.1 — Coincidence and union extension.** Split the printed theorem into uniqueness and union-extension declarations. Interpret coincidence as pointwise equality of $r,\phi,m$ on the intersection, and require the union solution to preserve precisely these three fields on each original domain. At BV regularity the auxiliary fields $\lambda,\nu,\alpha,\beta$, and hence the reconstructed conformal factor, depend on representative choices on null sets along characteristics. No pointwise equality of those auxiliary fields is part of the frozen conclusions.

**Theorem 5.1 — Family and threshold.** Express the printed premise for every $v_0\lt v_*$ as a family of C¹ solutions with the same prescribed data. Choose the universal $\varepsilon_1\gt0$ before the data, in accordance with the proof's choice $(5-2\sqrt6)/3$ on p. 1176. Express the limiting mass-ratio condition by one eventual bound $\mu\le\eta\lt\varepsilon_1$ on the shrinking triangles, simultaneously over all family members. The conclusion is existence of a solution with the same data on a larger development; an agreement clause with the input family is not in the frozen statement.

**Theorem 6.2 — Data and shared constant.** Permit arbitrary initial functions with $\operatorname{TV}_{(0,\infty)}(\alpha_0)\lt\varepsilon_*$, without a right-continuity assumption. Require the exact trace $\alpha(0,v)=\alpha_0(v)$ at every $v\gt0$. State the C¹ and BV existence results together, with a single positive $\varepsilon_*$ chosen before both data quantifiers, preserving the explicit link between Theorems 6.1 and 6.2.

**Scope — Sections 2, 7 and 8.** Sections 2, 7, and 8 are outside the present formalization scope. The selected theorem statements concern the reduced system and the existence, uniqueness, and continuation results described in the README; they do not claim a formalization of the entire paper.

**A — Lemma 6.2, extended-valued inequality (2026-10-17).** Retain the Fatou-type inequality for the nonnegative section-variation functionals, allowing the value $\infty$, without adding a finiteness hypothesis to repair the printed conclusion. The printed assertion that the limit functional belongs to L¹ does not follow from the printed hypotheses. The release includes the Lean counterexample `ChristodoulouBV.lemma6_2_L1_conclusion_false`.

**A′ — Lemma 6.2, lower integral (2026-10-18).** Use the lower Lebesgue integral inequality $\int^- F\le\liminf_n\int^- F_n$, without asserting measurability of $F$ or adding a measurability premise to the printed hypotheses; the theorem is `ChristodoulouBV.lemma6_2_lintegral`. Measurability needed for concrete section functionals in the existence argument must be proved separately. The printed lower-semicontinuity step is not valid in general, as shown by `ChristodoulouBV.lemma6_2_lsc_step_false`; this does not refute the adopted lower-integral inequality.
